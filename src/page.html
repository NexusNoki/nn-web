nn = nexus noki · open source

A smart home that answers to you.

nn runs your cameras, sensors and automations on boards you own, on your own network. No cloud account, no subscription, and no company that can switch your house off.

Why nn exists

Most smart homes are rented, not owned.

You buy the hardware, but the vendor keeps the keys. Four things go wrong again and again.

Your home lives on someone else's server

Cameras send your living room to a vendor's cloud. You can't see who looks at it, how long it is kept, or where it goes after a breach or a takeover.

The company closes, the device becomes a brick

When the servers shut down or the app leaves the store, hardware that still works stops working overnight. You paid for it; you can't keep it alive.

Broken, and nobody is allowed to fix it

Locked bootloaders, closed firmware, no spare parts. Once updates stop, security holes stay open, and the only repair left is the bin.

Lose the hub or the internet, lose the automations

A switch and a lamp in the same room, yet the decision travels to a data centre and back. When the link or the hub drops, the house stops listening.

Philosophy

Four promises behind every decision.

Each one answers one of those problems. They are the rules nn is built by, not features that can be switched off later.

Local first, no cloud

Video, sensor readings and automations stay in your home. The internet is optional: when it drops, cameras still record and lights still switch. Remote access is yours to add, behind your own login.

answers your home on someone else's server

You own the firmware

Every device runs firmware you can rebuild from public source and update from your own hub. No vendor account, no locked bootloader, no update server that can disappear.

answers the device becomes a brick

Devices act on their own

Automations are compiled on the hub and run on the sensors themselves, over the Thread mesh. A button still switches a lamp while the hub is off or restarting.

answers lose the hub, lose the automations

Cheap, off-the-shelf boards

ESP32 modules, a BeagleY-AI, an Orange Pi: boards anyone can buy. Replace a broken part yourself, and keep what you learn for your next project.

answers nobody is allowed to fix it
What nn does

Cameras, sensors and a hub that work together.

Cameras

Cameras that see, and keep what they see at home

Motion is checked five times a second. Only when something moves does the object detector wake up, on the camera's own AI accelerator or on the hub's, so the chip stays cool and the recordings stay yours.

  • Linux cameras (BeagleY-AI) and FreeRTOS cameras (ESP32-P4)
  • Live view in the browser, with event clips and detections
  • Swap in your own detection model without touching code
Sensors

Sensors that act on their own

Buttons, lights and sensors talk to each other over a Thread mesh radio. You write the rule in the hub's web app; the hub compiles it and hands it to the devices, which run it themselves.

  • No round trip to a server for a light switch
  • A Linux camera can double as the mesh gateway
  • The hub keeps the history and fills in what the radio missed
Hub

One hub, set up with one script

An Orange Pi 6 Plus or any Debian machine becomes the hub. It sets new devices up over Bluetooth, keeps their records, serves the web app and records camera events. Running the script again is the update.

  • Web app for devices, cameras, automations and logs
  • Flash a brand-new board straight from the browser
  • Look in from outside only if you choose, through your own tunnel and login
Updates

Updates you control, from your own hub

The hub signs every firmware image and sends it over the air. A device installs only an image it can verify, and goes back to the previous one by itself if the new one fails. You can rebuild every image from the public source.

  • Signed images, verified on the device
  • Automatic rollback when an update misbehaves
  • You decide when a version reaches the whole fleet
How it fits together

Everything talks inside your home.

The dashed path is the only one that leaves the house, and it exists only if you add it.

Solid lines stay on your network; the dotted amber lines are the Thread radio mesh. Cameras send video to the media server; the hub keeps the control channel and its keys never leave it.

Hardware

No proprietary hub and no special radios. The manual walks you through each one.
